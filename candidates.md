# Candidates — Office Artifacts: The One-Line Placard Contest

Brief: one line, at most 180 characters, a museum placard for an ordinary
office object written as if it were an ancient artifact. Judge: a dry-witted
curator model (z-ai/glm-5.3-flash via the house call), pairwise, no ties.

Scoring below follows the judge brief's criteria **in the brief's order**,
each /5, total /25:

1. **C1 Placard & premise** — a one-line placard for an office object treated
   as an ancient artifact, inventive and funny.
2. **C2 Conceit** — a specific, surprising imagined history, not a generic
   office joke.
3. **C3 Dryness** — deadpan curator's voice; the joke lands without straining
   or explaining itself.
4. **C4 Economy** — every word earns its place; reads well aloud.
5. **C5 Label craft** — catalogue details (date, material, provenance, donor)
   used to sell the joke.

## Knockout — top 8, head-to-head in the judge's criterion order

Quarter-finals (first differing criterion decides, per the brief):

- **#1 (24) vs #8 (20)** → #1 wins: C2 — "fasten loose prayers of paper" is a
  fuller invented rite than the marker's unfinished sentence; C5 also to #1
  (date + material + named donor vs date + material only).
- **#4 (22) vs #5 (22)** → #4 wins: tie on totals broken on C2 — the scribe
  *demanding letters no priest could supply* is the more surprising history;
  the fish law is funnier dry (C3 to #5) but by less than the C2 gap.
- **#2 (23) vs #7 (21)** → #2 wins: C2 — named prophecies (BUY MILK, CALL
  DENISE) are the most specific imagined history in the field.
- **#3 (22) vs #6 (21)** → #3 wins: C2 — the lost-moons rite beats the chair's
  bad-news squeak; C3 close, C5 to #3.

Semi-finals:

- **#1 vs #4** → #1 wins: C3 — "still bites" lands deadpan in two words; the
  scribe's joke needs its demand spelled out. C5 level (both full labels).
- **#2 vs #3** → #2 wins: C3 — "honoured, then stuck to a monitor until it
  fell" is drier; C2 level at the top end.

Final:

- **#1 vs #2** → **#1 wins.** C1 level, C2 level (both histories specific).
  Decided on **C5, label craft**: #1 carries date, material *and* a donor
  whose joke ("who asked not to be named") is itself the driest line in
  either placard, while #2's donor is merely "unknown". C3 also shades to
  #1 — "still bites" needs no explanation at all.
